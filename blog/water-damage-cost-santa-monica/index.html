How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Santa Monica?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Santa Monica?

The Quick Answer

In Santa Monica, the usual water damage bill lands in a range of $1,500 to $10,000 or more. Small clean-water jobs can come in under about $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. What labor and materials cost in Santa Monica, CA will shift the total higher or lower. For a precise figure, there is no substitute for moisture readings taken during an on-site visit.

Cost by Water Category

The first thing a restoration estimate looks at is what kind of water it is. Contamination level places every loss in one of three industry categories. As contamination rises, so does the need for cleaning, tear-out of materials and protective equipment.

Across the US, cleanup tends to run roughly $3 to $7.50 per square foot depending on category. The table below shows common rates.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterWater heater tanks, faucets and supply linesRoughly $3 to $4
Category 2, gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, sump pump failuresAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3, black waterStorm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

The category is not fixed. Leave clean water standing a couple of days and bacteria grow, so it gets handled as black or gray water. It is part of why delay makes a job more expensive.

What Drives the Price

Two homes with the same broken pipe can get very different bills. Here is what a contractor looks at when pricing a job.

  • Area affected. Labor, equipment and time on site all grow with the amount of wet square footage.
  • Water category. Clean water is the cheapest to handle. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. The longer water sits, the deeper it gets into walls and the subfloor. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Concrete and tile are easy to dry. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Existing growth means remediation work, including containment and filtered air, becomes part of the job.
  • Reconstruction. Drying is one cost. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

Estimates usually cover a pair of phases, extraction of the water and then drying of the structure. In general the drying phase lasts 3 to 5 days.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are often billed by the day. Three days of drying is cheaper than six.

Price Ranges by Type of Job

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in Santa Monica. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. A pipe that ran for hours, or wet more than one level, costs more.
  • Appliance leak. Catch a leaking water heater, washing machine or dishwasher early and the bill is often $1,000 to $4,000. Hidden damage makes a slow leak beneath a cabinet more expensive.
  • Basement flood. Typically $2,000 to $10,000 or more, depending on depth, the water source and whether the space is finished. Finished basements land near the top of the range for basement water removal.
  • Sewage backup. A common range is $3,000 to $10,000 or higher. Sewage cleanup is Category 3 work, so porous materials are removed and everything is disinfected.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in Santa Monica?

Two things decide it: the cause of the loss and your policy's wording. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Unless you have a separate flood policy, water that enters from outside, like storm surge or a rising river, is usually excluded. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Remember to consider your deductible. A small repair may come in under the deductible, in which case filing may not be worthwhile.

Ways to Lower the Cost

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water keeps spreading until it is removed. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. Know where your main valve is and close it as soon as you find a leak.
  • Document everything. Shoot video and pictures first, while everything is still in place and nothing has been tossed. Save every receipt.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. Once it does, the job gets longer and more expensive.
  • Move what you can. Furniture, rugs and boxes should come up off wet flooring, provided that is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. When outside water is the source, flood cleanup belongs with a crew that has proper protective equipment.

Getting a Real Number

National ranges are useful for planning, but your home is not an average. To learn the true spread of the water, and the work required to dry and fix it, a technician has to inspect with a meter.

The smart move is to schedule that inspection today, before the damage grows, with a water damage restoration company in Santa Monica, CA.
